# Concept Diversity Enforcement

When generating concepts for an opportunity, they must be materially different - not cosmetic variations of the same idea.

## Mechanism Categories

Each concept must use a different mechanism from this list:

1. **Trust signal** - Surface information that helps users judge quality/reliability
2. **Ranking / aggregation** - Change how items are sorted, grouped, or filtered
3. **Workflow change** - Alter the steps a user takes to accomplish their goal
4. **Manual service layer** - Add human-powered service on top of the product
5. **Data layer** - Add new data sources or enrich existing data
6. **AI assist** - Use ML/AI to automate judgment, prediction, or generation
7. **Policy / rules change** - Change platform rules, incentives, or enforcement
8. **Pricing / incentive change** - Use economic levers to change behavior
9. **Communication channel** - Add or change how/when users are notified or contacted
10. **UX reduction** - Remove steps, screens, or decisions rather than adding features

## How to Check

When generating alternatives, tag their mechanisms and check that the proposed actions differ materially. Three concepts is a starting point, not a quota. A shared category can still contain different mechanisms; explain the difference rather than discard a useful option to fill a category. Preserve selected concepts when resuming experiment design.

## Examples of Cosmetic vs. Material Diversity

**Bad (cosmetic):**
- Concept A: Add a green checkmark badge for verified listings (trust signal)
- Concept B: Add a "Verified" text label for verified listings (trust signal)
- Concept C: Add a trust score number for verified listings (trust signal)

All three are the same idea wearing different clothes.

**Good (material):**
- Concept A: Truth Strip showing days-on-market and price history (trust signal)
- Concept B: Cluster duplicate listings into one result with agent comparison (ranking/aggregation)
- Concept C: Agent reputation card with response rate and accuracy score (data layer)

Three genuinely different approaches to the same trust opportunity.

## Why This Matters

Cosmetic diversity creates the illusion of choice. You think you explored the space, but you only explored one corner. Material diversity forces you to consider solutions you wouldn't naturally reach for - and those are often the best ones.
