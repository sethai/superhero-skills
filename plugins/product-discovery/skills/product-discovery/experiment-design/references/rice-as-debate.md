# Compare Experiments by the Decision They Enable

## Separate two decisions

- **What to learn next:** which uncertainty is worth reducing now, with the smallest useful investment?
- **What to develop or launch:** which product idea has enough relevant evidence for a particular implementation commitment?

A test can inform the first without settling the second. State the decision's scope before comparing methods. Demand, task behavior, experienced value and payment are different signals; their percentages cannot directly rank competing product ideas.

## Compare the portfolio

For each plausible test, explain:

1. What next action its result could change and why that decision matters.
2. Which important uncertainty it reduces, and which uncertainties remain.
3. How the method fits the assumption and the existing evidence, including counterevidence.
4. Actual preparation, available materials, participant access and dependencies.
5. Time until useful learning, including recruitment or the natural opportunity to use/buy again.

Use a compact comparison table and recommend a next step. Retain other relevant tests as alternatives or conditional follow-ups. Parallel execution is an option, not a default. If access, cost or timing is unknown, name that dependency rather than invent estimates.

RICE dimensions may provide vocabulary for reach, impact, confidence and effort when useful; do not calculate a synthetic score. Neither source count nor a vivid quote automatically overrides evidence fit or the decision's importance.

## Make results actionable

Each test needs favorable, unfavorable and mixed/insufficient evidence patterns linked to concrete proposed actions. Do not make the reader infer the decision from a metric alone.

- Favorable evidence justifies only the next investment supported by the test's scope, not an automatic rollout.
- An unfavorable result can reflect the format, missing information, exposure or the underlying idea. Identify the diagnosis rather than automatically selecting another idea.
- Mixed evidence requires inspecting the relevant tradeoff, subgroup or measurement gap. Do not force a binary verdict.

## Direction before thresholds

Default to what should improve or be observed and how it will be measured. The human decides whether the evidence is sufficient. Do not invent numerical targets to make the plan look complete. Record human assessment separately from the agent's recommendation.

When the user supplies numerical criteria, preserve their provenance. When asked to propose criteria, explain the basis in the decision's consequences, baseline, cost or minimum useful effect and mark proposals as unaccepted until agreed. If that basis is unavailable, propose obtaining it. Example percentages, qualitative counts and task times are not reusable benchmarks.
