# Focused market research and deliverable

Start research after the bundled opening, before the candidate table in assistant
message 2-3. Research competitors/alternatives, rough size/buyer counts, typical payers,
and prices yourself, using web search and source reading when available. Ask the user
only about their experience, access, constraints, incidents, and preferences, never
to perform public market research. Reuse evidence across candidates and refine only
decisive unknowns for the chosen direction. Attempt each required gate dimension;
keep sourcing gaps visible with their decision effect. Research should shorten the
interview, not postpone a feasible comparison or create a long report by default.
Sections 1-5 are selective research inputs; the six-point gate below is mandatory.
Time pressure or a request to shorten/get the result triggers the provisional close
in that same reply; it takes priority over intake, table selection, and confirmation.

For each thesis, identify what finding would support or defeat it before researching.
Prioritize evidence about actual spending, unmet outcomes, buying behavior, and the
constraint leaving room for entry. Connect findings to those premises. A competitive
catalogue or broad adoption statistic alone does not explain a business opportunity.

## Evidence and sources

Open sources supporting decisive claims. Prefer official statistics, registries,
studies with stated methods, public filings, verified company pricing, and first-hand
accounts for the relevant fact. Give direct links and dates beside findings. Describe
vendor claims as vendor claims and inspect survey scope, samples, and methods.

Match evidence to the specific claim: observed pain, its cause, prevalence,
willingness to pay, and demand for this solution are different claims. An anecdote
establishes an instance; job postings suggest recruitment activity; general complaints
about a department do not establish a specific information gap. Broad sector size
cannot repair missing problem evidence. If that gap determines the verdict, ask only
for decisive first-hand incident detail or propose an outside-chat test; do not restart
intake or preserve a candidate based on a founder-strength example.

For inaccessible sources, try another primary source or an explicitly qualified
secondary source. Bound the search; explain remaining limitations instead of inventing
data. Without browsing, inspect supplied sources or label the research gate incomplete;
unverified hypotheses and a research plan cannot substitute for completed research.

## 1. Customer counts and market value

In every early candidate row, show rough buyer size labeled **estimate** with source
and assumptions, or an explicit sourcing gap if credible inputs are unavailable.
Before final confirmation, always attempt the chosen direction's buyer estimate.
Use ranges rather than invented precision. Without credible inputs, keep status
provisional; never invent a count to fill the table or ask the user to research it.
Define customer, buyer, offer, and geography. Seek counts for the actual segment rather
than its umbrella industry. State the data year, unit, coverage, and exclusions:
locations versus companies or purchasing groups, public/private boundaries, employer
size, active versus registered entities. Avoid double-counting overlapping registries.
Several locations under one central buyer need not represent several buying decisions.

Estimate value from eligible paying customers × plausible spend × purchase frequency,
showing inputs, units, sources, and assumptions. Separate TAM, realistically served
SAM, and an initial sales scenario. One-time purchases and recurring spend use different
time bases. A broad reference bound or sensitivity scenario is not an established TAM
or SAM; explain missing segmentation. Derive early sales from reachable buyers and
capacity rather than an arbitrary percentage of the industry.

## 2. Extent and cost of the problem

Seek evidence beyond the founder's example: operational studies, surveys, independent
accounts from multiple organizations, observed spending, or procurement. Describe who
is affected, sample or scope, period, frequency, and consequences when known. Preserve
proxy limitations and unknown causes. Workforce shortages do not by themselves prove
training gaps, and repeated frustration alone does not establish willingness to buy.

## 3. Relevant sector conditions

Choose material forces such as wages, labor availability, budgets, demographics,
growth, consolidation, technology adoption, or rules. For each, cite dated evidence
and explain the mechanism affecting this buyer, need, or budget. Include headwinds.
Distinguish historical observations, forecasts, and inferred effects; a growing sector
does not establish demand for every offer in it.

## 4. Alternatives and buying

Compare manual work, internal teams, services, and direct competitors. Verify public
prices, scope, tax basis, billing unit, and date; listed offers are not sales evidence.
Use the specific insight and prior-attempt questions from problem discovery to assess
an advantage and the strongest objection. Explain the budget owner, purchase process,
and realistic route to the buyer. A segment change requires a fresh access assessment.

Competing businesses with paying customers provide positive evidence of category
demand, budgets, and buying routes. Identify what customers buy and why; distinguish
observed purchases from a provider's listed offer. Explore how a new entrant could
be a better choice for a specific buyer, including tradeoffs and alternative models.

Summarize the closest alternatives and propose grounded differences as labeled
hypotheses for the user to pick/edit. Ask only about unique assets, incidents, or
preferences that could change the verdict. Use `differentiation-and-moats.md` selectively;
no separate differentiation interview or unaided answer is required. Assess
the difference's customer value, starting asset, acquisition path, and possible
mechanism for sustaining it. Match the strength of the claim to the evidence: an
initial wedge, a buildable advantage, and an established moat are different stages.

Inspect copying and bundling as competitive risks, not automatic vetoes. An incumbent
being able to add a feature does not establish that customers will choose its offer
or that a new entrant cannot build a valuable business. Reject a thesis when its
business premise fails or, after exploring differences, there is no plausible reason
to buy. Keep consequential untested differentiation unresolved. Market size and
subscription arithmetic still cannot establish a customer's reason to choose.

## 5. Economics and scaling

During the normal interview, before suggesting ideas, offers, groups, or formats, know willingness to
sell/promote, budget, weekly hours, skills (including coding), and income goal. Use
the bundled opening; gather skipped prerequisites in the next single block of 2-3
questions before ideas. A conditional scenario or "just give me an idea" does not
waive fit. Never assume a skipped answer. For credible candidates, show labeled price,
frequency, delivery time, capacity, and cost assumptions against those constraints. Recheck the
chosen mechanism before final wording approval.
Distinguish revenue, cash remaining after costs, and owner income; expose omitted
costs and the treatment of founder labor. Market size alone cannot prove profitability.

Assess initial feasibility separately from the intended model. Identify the unit sold,
what is reused, and what remains bespoke. Explain the growth mechanism: self-service,
group delivery, automation, licensing, distribution, or a team, and evidence supporting
it, including the critical assumption. Recording a course, adding a subscription, or
proposing future hires alone does not establish scalable delivery. Recurring payment
needs recurring customer value.

Compare suitable smaller and larger customer counts, for example 10 versus 100.
Account for setup investment, founder hours, sales and acquisition, customization,
onboarding, maintenance and updates, support, retention, and material costs. Include
wages, supervision, and quality control if delegation drives growth. Explain whether
delivery hours or expensive human labor grow proportionally with revenue and what
would need to change. Efficient delivery alone is not passive income.

Hands-on early work can build access and understanding. A manual pilot can also test
a repeatable problem, a common solution, or purchase of the intended model. Name its
learning or reusable asset, what would contradict repeatability, and what remains
untested. Payment for bespoke consulting does not validate self-service demand.
If founder-led work is required anew for each customer, lower its rank for a scalable
business goal or return to other problems; adding a hypothetical app is insufficient.

## Mandatory gate: Is it big enough?

Before asking for final wording confirmation, assess all six points for the chosen
direction. Use a compact evidence table; reuse findings instead of restarting research.

1. **Who pays:** name the buyer/budget owner and the person or group suffering the
   problem. When they differ, explicitly write **"Payer ≠ sufferer"** in the conversation
   language, name both parties, and explain why the payer would act. Label an untested
   reason as a hypothesis; never transfer sufferer pain to the payer by assumption.
   Apply this explicit distinction in early quick checks as well as the full gate.
2. **Cost to the payer:** state time, money, and risk separately, each labeled
   **evidence** (with source and scope) or **assumption** (with basis or unknown).
   Show units and period for estimates. Mark unknown costs explicitly; do not invent
   amounts or treat the sufferer's lost time as a payer cost without a causal link.
3. **How many buyers:** give a rough count or range for such buying entities in the
   relevant segment and geography, labeled **estimate**, with sources, dates, and
   assumptions. Show the calculation; count budget decisions, not merely users.
   If credible inputs cannot be found, report the sourcing attempt and gap, not a
   fabricated number; the sizing gate stays incomplete and the direction provisional.
4. **Recurrence:** state how often the problem occurs and whether it repeats for
   the same buyer or across new buyers. Distinguish observed frequency from assumptions;
   a one-time problem does not become recurring value through subscription pricing.
5. **Why alternatives are insufficient:** name the researched competitors and actual
   alternatives (including manual work or doing nothing), with sources. Explain the
   remaining gap for this buyer and supporting evidence or an untested hypothesis.
   If no meaningful gap is supported, say so; do not invent competitor weaknesses.
6. **Repeatability beyond the founder:** explain whether a repeatable product can
   deliver value without the founder's personal labor for each customer. Identify
   reused assets, delivery/support/customization needs, evidence, and untested steps.
   Separate a manual learning pilot from the intended operating model. Delegating
   bespoke labor may support a service business; it does not alone prove a repeatable
   product, and a hypothetical future app does not establish independence.

Give exactly one verdict, using one of these labels verbatim (even in a translated
conversation; give reasons in the user's language). These are alternative conclusions,
not stages to promote the idea through:

| Verdict | Basis for using it |
| --- | --- |
| company/product with growth potential | Credible payer value, buyer pool and demand, a reason to choose over alternatives, and a plausible repeatable delivery and growth path. State what remains untested; potential is not proven growth. |
| small business / service | A credible paid offer whose size, economics, or dependence on human delivery supports a limited business or service. It can suit a solo-practice goal; do not inflate its growth claim. |
| feature in someone else's product | Evidence supports a useful capability but its standalone buying case is weak and it fits an existing product or workflow. Incumbent copyability alone is insufficient for this verdict. |
| learning project | The supported value is mainly learning or practice, with no credible commercial case currently established. Missing research alone is not evidence for this verdict. |
| not yet known | Consequential evidence is missing or conflicting, so a stronger classification would be speculation. Name the decisive gap. |

Beside every candidate, refined, or provisional super sentence, show the verdict,
its evidence, key assumptions/gaps, reasons, and what evidence would change it; keep this outside
the fixed template. An early draft can use "not yet known" with the limited evidence
available and missing checks; it need not wait for full research. Never inflate the
verdict to match enthusiasm, ambition, elegant wording, or pressure to finish.
A wording-only re-display after the single final confirmation request inherits the
previous verdict; show only the edited sentence, not the package again.

For "feature in someone else's product" or "learning project", say that conclusion
plainly and recommend whether to keep searching and where: a specific adjacent buyer,
costly recurring outcome, spending pool, or accessible environment. If continuing as
a feature or learning exercise suits the user's goal, say why; do not silently confirm
it as a business recommendation. Wording approval does not upgrade the verdict.

On time pressure or a request to shorten/get the result, immediately deliver the
provisional close in that same reply: a grounded literal-template super sentence,
one honest verdict with brief basis from all six findings/unknowns, and one concrete
outside-chat action with participant/source type, timebox, and proposed pass criterion.
No further questions or confirmation; a side artifact such as outreach copy cannot
replace this result. Preserve supported verdicts; list skipped work and decisive gaps.
Incomplete research or unresolved decisive premises still prohibit final status,
even after wording approval. If no
credible idea emerged, give the honest verdict and next search target without
manufacturing a sentence.

## Final deliverable and single confirmation

The main deliverable is the refined super sentence in `rpt-interview.md`'s literal
four-part template, fixed lead phrases verbatim and in order. Each slot is one short
clause, roughly at most 50 words overall. Outcome means a result for people. The
"We want them to be able to [change]" slot states only a positive capability/change
covering every who-group; no means ("thanks to X", "by using X", "dzięki ...", "przez ...",
"z pomocą ..."). Put means in the approach/mechanism slot. Run that check before every
presentation. Keep buyer, economics, differentiation, evidence, and verdict outside it.
Prefer a supported who-group that experiences the problem and can act on or adopt the
solution. If only sufferers unable to adopt are grounded, state that limitation and
explain the adoption route with assumptions/gaps. Keep the explicit payer/sufferer
distinction and coverage check; never invent payer pain to make the payer fit who.
Use the local artifact name consistently and keep supporting reasoning concise.

Present one review package: refined sentence, constraint fit, full six-point gate,
exactly one verdict, evidence, assumptions/gaps, and one next action. Reuse early
research; do not require a new report or exhaustive analysis of every optional lens.
Ask a single time to confirm or edit. Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation
the skill, its files, paths, rules, or English instructions to the user; just ask in
the conversation language. On confirmation, close briefly without repeating the
sentence, gate, or question. Wording-only edits require only the edited sentence,
inheriting the previous verdict; do not request confirmation again. Substantive edits
require reassessing affected premises and status, without restarting the interview
or asking for final confirmation again. Wording approval does not validate a business.

Aim for about 6-8 user replies, sooner when ready. At roughly eight replies, unresolved
work gets a provisional best grounded sentence with verdict "not yet known", decisive
gaps, the six gate findings/unknowns, and one concrete action outside chat. Specify
participant/source type, timebox, and proposed pass criterion. For example, propose
five interviews with a named buyer type within two weeks; proceed only if a stated
number show a recent costly incident and an existing budget or workaround. These are
proposed decision thresholds, not market facts or completed interviews. Do not add
another confirmation request at this provisional close. Extend only at the user's request.
Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just deliver the close in the conversation language.

If no credible idea emerges, give the honest status, rejection reasons, and one concrete
next search action with the same participant/source type, timebox, and pass-criterion
detail instead of manufacturing a sentence. A feature or learning-project
verdict stays explicit; explain whether continuing fits the user's goal or where to
search next. A profitable niche can be a valid result. Missing evidence is neither
proof of failure nor proof of potential.

Research supports hypotheses, not proof of product-market fit or willingness to pay.
Offer the result in chat; save a file when requested or useful in the host's artifact
workflow. External actions require separate authorization as stated in SKILL.md.
