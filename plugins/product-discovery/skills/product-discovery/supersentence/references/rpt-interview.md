# Super sentence interview inspired by Radical Product Thinking

Use to identify a meaningful customer change before selecting a business. The
who/what/why/when/how foundation comes from Radhika Dutt's
[mission statement article](https://www.radicalproduct.com/blog/how-to-write-a-mission-statement),
published November 16, 2022. Causal probing and the later commercial assessment are
our additions. Use the fixed template below; do not copy the article or toolkit.

Use `interview.md`'s single bundled opening and candidate table by assistant message
2-3. These prompts are a selective menu, not a sequence. Reuse known answers and
research public facts yourself. During the normal interview, know constraints before
ideas. After the opening, ask only about verdict-changing gaps in at most one grouped block of 2-3
questions per turn. A user pick/edit resolves direction; do not add intermediate
wording confirmations. Aim for about 6-8 user replies, sooner when ready.
Time pressure or a request to shorten/get the result overrides intake, candidate
selection, and confirmation: deliver the provisional close below in that same reply.
For the opening and candidate table, never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation
the skill, its files, paths, rules, or English instructions to the user; just do the
step in the conversation language.

## People and desired outcome

- "Whose world would you like to change?"
- "Which people, in what particular situation, do you mean?"
- "What contact do you have with them?"
- "Why does this change matter to you?"
- "What are they trying to achieve in that situation?"

Define the group by recognizable circumstances or behavior when useful. A broad
job title or population is a starting point. Check the founder's access, motivation,
and relevant capabilities through `interview.md`. Reframe a preferred product as
the outcome it should enable without inventing an unmet need for it. The outcome
slot describes a result or state for people, never a solution element, product, plan,
or tool; keep those in the mechanism slot.
Prefer a grounded group that both experiences the problem and can act on or adopt the
solution. For example, a manager losing time to shift gaps may fit better than staff
unable to adopt a scheduling tool, if the manager's problem is supported. Budget
authority alone is insufficient. If only sufferers unable to adopt are grounded,
say so and explain who would enable adoption and how, labeling assumptions/gaps.
Still explicitly flag "Payer ≠ sufferer" with both named whenever they differ.

## Current arrangements and obstacles

- "How do they handle this today?"
- "Tell me about the last time this happened."
- "What happened as a result?"
- "What specifically prevented the outcome in that case?"
- "What else could explain what happened?"
- "What would help us distinguish those explanations?"

Separate symptom, cause, and consequence. A desire for improvement establishes none
of them by itself. A requested app or course may conceal an obstacle in capacity,
information, trust, incentives, coordination, affordability, or the physical process.
Use these as possible explanations, not leading diagnoses. Establish what existing
attempts changed and what they left unresolved. One instance remains one instance.

Give supported explanations their evidence; keep other explanations as hypotheses.
If the cause is unknown, draft the desired change with that uncertainty outside the
statement and choose a causal learning action before recommending a specific fix.
Avoid turning an untested explanation into the customer's universal situation.

## Significance and tangible end state

- "Why is this situation important enough to change?"
- "Imagine it is resolved. What happens differently on an ordinary day?"
- "What would the person be able to do then?"

Look for a concrete scene, decision, or capability instead of an inspirational slogan.
Include consequences for other people affected when relevant. Describe success in
the customer's terms, not founder revenue, company leadership, or valuation. Keep
KPIs and experimental thresholds outside the super sentence; use them later to test it.
A feasible end state can acknowledge tradeoffs rather than promise perfection.
Phrase the change slot as a positive capability or change in the world, not an
absence such as "without X" or "bez ...". Say what each who-group will be able to do.

## Provisional mechanism and fit

- "What part of this change can you help bring about?"
- "How would your approach remove the obstacle we identified?"
- "What would have to be true for it to work?"

A mechanism explains how change happens. It can remain provisional without selecting
a course, app, marketplace, or consulting offer. Compare plausible approaches when
they solve different causes and fit the user's access, interests, strengths, and
limits. In the normal interview, complete suggestion prerequisites before offering an approach.
Reuse the early candidate table rather than restarting selection. Each candidate has
a literal-template working sentence and quick big-enough check, including payer versus
sufferer, estimated buyer size with basis/source or sourcing gap, main alternative,
and one permitted verdict. If payer and sufferer differ, explicitly say "Payer ≠ sufferer"
in the conversation language, name both, and explain why the payer would act. Preserve
sourcing gaps rather than invent counts. User picks/edits guide material scope changes.

## Draft, refine, confirm, and finalize the super sentence

Draft candidate sentences early from grounded people, desired change, current
arrangements/workarounds, obstacles, and consequences; keep missing evidence explicit
beside them. Clearly label each a working draft. Every draft and final version must
use these four parts in this order.
Fill only bracketed slots; preserve every lead phrase verbatim:

> [People in a specific situation] need [outcome]. Their current arrangements leave
> them facing [problem, including current workarounds], with [consequence]. We want
> them to be able to [change in the world]. Our initial approach is [mechanism].

Write in the user's conversation language. Use this exact Polish template:

> [Kto, w jakiej sytuacji] potrzebują [rezultat]. Obecne rozwiązania zostawiają ich z
> [problem, obecne obejścia], przez co [konsekwencja]. Chcemy, żeby mogli [zmiana w
> świecie]. Nasze podejście to [mechanizm].

For other languages, translate the fixed lead phrases once and reuse that exact
translation every time; do not paraphrase. Each slot is one short clause; keep the
whole statement roughly at most 50 words. Put supporting detail beside it.

Label material causal assumptions and missing evidence beside the draft. Show one
honest verdict and its evidence beside candidate/refined/provisional versions, following
`market-and-output.md`. A wording-only re-display inherits the previous verdict and shows
only the edited sentence. An early draft may use "not yet known" with known evidence
and remaining gaps. Keep an
unknown mechanism explicit rather than inventing one. Let candidate selection and
user edits resolve interpretations; do not request intermediate wording confirmation.

Use the draft as a filter: which activities advance the change, which do not, and
what apparent improvement could undermine it? Refine that same statement through
further interview, business hypotheses, research, and commercial assessment. Once
an offer is chosen, name that offer and how it works in the mechanism slot. Revisit
material changes through user picks/edits; do not silently substitute a new customer
or problem or add another confirmation loop.

Keep buyer, differentiation, moat, economics, business viability, income goals, and
commercial evidence as supporting material beside the super sentence. A meaningful
difference may shape the mechanism wording only where established; never invent one.
A compelling super sentence is a direction, not proof of need, willingness to pay,
or a moat. Do not turn an aspiration into a proven result.

Before presenting any version, check:

- All fixed phrases are present verbatim and in order, with only slots filled.
- Each slot is one short clause; the whole statement is roughly at most 50 words.
- The who-group experiences the supported problem and preferably can act on or adopt
  the solution. If only sufferers unable to adopt are grounded, their limitation and
  adoption route are explicit beside the sentence. Payer and sufferer are named with
  "Payer ≠ sufferer" when different; payer pain is never inferred. Any material change
  of who follows user picks/edits rather than silent substitution.
- The outcome is a result for people, never a solution element, product, plan, or tool.
- The "We want them to be able to [change]" end state ("Chcemy, żeby mogli [...]")
  states only a positive capability/change covering every group in the who slot.
  Never put a mechanism or means here: no "thanks to X", "by using X", "dzięki ...",
  "przez ...", or "z pomocą ...". Move means to the approach/mechanism slot; then
  re-check that the capability stands on its own. Avoid absence-based end states
  ("without X", "bez ..."). If coverage fails, propose narrowing who for the user
  to pick/edit; never invent an end state for another group.
- Customer, supported problem with concrete workarounds, consequence, change, and
  mechanism match the assessment. Avoid slogans, generic AI claims, and product lists.
- The artifact name stays in the user's language: "super sentence" in English,
  "superzdanie" in Polish, one consistent local name elsewhere.
- In gendered languages, user forms stay neutral until gender is known; assistant
  self-reference stays neutral and consistent, as in `interview.md`.
- Exactly one permitted verdict appears beside candidate/refined/provisional sentences
  with evidence, reasons, assumptions/gaps, and what evidence would change it. A wording-only
  re-display inherits that verdict and shows only the sentence. Before final confirmation,
  all six "Is it big enough?" checks are assessed: payer versus sufferer, payer's
  time/money/risk costs labeled evidence or assumption, sourced buyer estimate,
  recurrence, named researched alternatives and remaining gap, and repeatability
  without the founder's personal labor. Missing decisive evidence means provisional.
  For a feature or learning-project verdict, state it plainly and recommend whether
  to keep searching and where; wording approval must not imply business validation.

## Single final confirmation and closure

Before the one final confirmation, always complete this gate in order:

1. Briefly research alternatives/competitors, rough buyer counts, typical payers,
   and prices; use web search when available. Without browsing, inspect supplied
   sources; a research plan alone leaves this undone.
2. Check the mechanism against willingness to sell/promote, budget, weekly hours,
   skills including coding, income goal, and other known limits. Bundle consequential
   user-specific unknowns; research public unknowns yourself.
3. Assess all six "Is it big enough?" points from `market-and-output.md` in a compact
   table. For different parties, explicitly write "Payer ≠ sufferer" in the conversation
   language, name both, and give the payer's reason to act. Label its uncertainty.
4. Present the refined sentence with exactly one permitted verdict, evidence, reasons,
   key assumptions/gaps, material qualifications, and one concrete next step beside it.
   Keep commercial analysis outside the literal template. Run all pre-presentation checks.
5. Ask a single time to confirm or edit. Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation
   the skill, its files, paths, rules, or English instructions to the user; just ask in
   the conversation language. On confirmation, close with a brief acknowledgment;
   do not repeat the sentence, package, or question. For a wording-only edit, re-show
   only the edited sentence, inheriting the previous verdict; no renewed confirmation.
   For a substantive change, reassess affected premises and status without restarting
   the full package or asking for final confirmation again. Do not silently change scope.

Wording approval never upgrades evidence. Incomplete research or unresolved decisive
premises mean provisional status; list the gaps briefly. If the user signals time
pressure, asks to shorten, or asks for the sentence/result, deliver the provisional
close immediately in that same reply: the best grounded literal-template sentence,
one honest verdict with brief basis from all six gate findings/unknowns, and one
concrete outside-chat action with participant/source type, timebox, and proposed pass
criterion. No further questions or confirmation; outreach copy or another side artifact
cannot replace this result. Preserve supported verdicts and the no-credible-idea
exception below, rather than inventing facts or a sentence.
At about eight user replies, stop probing unresolved work: give the best grounded
sentence as provisional with verdict "not yet known" and one outside-chat action with
a reachable participant/source type, timebox, and proposed pass criterion. Do not add
a confirmation request at this close. Extend only if the user explicitly wants more.
Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just deliver the close in the conversation language.
If no credible idea emerged, explain that honest result and one next search action
with the same participant/source type, timebox, and pass-criterion detail instead of
fabricating a sentence. A feature or learning-project conclusion
must stay explicit; elegant wording does not imply business potential.
