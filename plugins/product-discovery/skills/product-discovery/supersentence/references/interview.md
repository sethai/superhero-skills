# Compact profile interview

Use one bundled opening, then only verdict-changing follow-ups. Reuse supplied answers;
prompt examples below are a menu, not separate turns or a mandatory profile survey.

## Opening message

Briefly explain the path: compare grounded options, research them, refine one sentence.
Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just do the opening in the conversation language.
Use one numbered question block, shortened to omit anything already supplied. For example:

1. What work do you do now, and what jobs have you done before?
2. Any side jobs, occasional paid work, or volunteering?
3. Which hobbies or communities do you spend time in or know from the inside?
4. What frustrated you recently: who was involved, what happened, and what did you try?
5. What weekly hours and budget can you commit, and how willing are you to sell/promote?
6. What skills can you use, including coding, and what income goal would make this worthwhile?

Say that a few lines are enough and items may be skipped. Do not split these into
one-question turns, repeat answered items, or add another question block to the opening.
Existing first-hand context can substitute for an item; do not require a ritual retelling.
Studies, family businesses, and unpaid work can provide relevant grounding too.

During the normal interview, know constraints before suggesting ideas, offers, groups, or formats:
weekly hours, budget, willingness to sell/promote, skills including coding, and income goal.
Skipping is allowed; skipped constraints remain unknown. In the next single grouped block
of 2-3 questions, collect missing prerequisites and decisive grounding, without proposing
ideas first. Reuse known limits even for "just give me an idea"; briefly explain fit.
Do not assume resources, selling capacity, or permission to use employer materials.

## Candidate table by assistant message 2-3

Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just present the table in the conversation language.
Research competitors/alternatives, rough size/buyer counts, typical payers, and prices
yourself, using web search when available. Keep estimates and sources visible; an
unavailable count is a sourcing gap, never a made-up number. Ask the user only for
experience, access, constraints, incidents, and preferences that only they know.

Present 2-3 distinct grounded problem spaces in one table, even with a favorite or
low-engagement user. Each row needs:

- A working super sentence with all four literal RPT parts and fixed lead phrases.
- Access/problem evidence and fit to known constraints, distinguishing hypotheses.
- Quick big-enough check: payer and sufferer, rough buyer size labeled estimate with
  source/basis or sourcing gap, main alternative, exactly one permitted verdict with
  evidence and the decisive uncertainty. Include researched prices/payers where useful.
- If different, explicitly say "Payer ≠ sufferer" in the conversation language, name
  both, and state the payer's reason to act, labeling untested value as a hypothesis.
- Prefer a supported who-group that experiences the problem and can act on or adopt
  the solution. If only sufferers unable to adopt are grounded, state that limitation
  and explain who would enable adoption and how, with assumptions/gaps explicit.

Ask which row to pick or edit. Do not ask the user to construct the comparison or
supply market research. One environment can contain several distinct supported problems;
variants of one solution are not distinct problem spaces. Ground groups and pain in
supplied context or sourced observations within demonstrated environments. Do not
invent unrelated groups, demand, pain, or counts to fill the table.

If grounding is too thin for two candidates, say so and ask one grouped block of 2-3
questions for missing grounding (and unknown constraints). Research within accessible
environments can help, but cannot prove the user's access. Keep selection pending;
a lone supported idea is not automatically a winner. Do not defer a feasible table
past message 3 just to complete a profile. If prerequisites remain missing, state the
reason for the delay and use the soft limit rather than another long intake.

## Selective follow-ups after the pick

Ask only when the answer could change the verdict or viability; at most one grouped
block of 2-3 questions per turn. Do not traverse these menus in order:

- **Access:** Who can you realistically reach in the next two weeks? Which resources
  can you legitimately use? Research buying roles; ask about personal relationships.
- **Interests:** Which delivery work would you enjoy repeating, and what would you avoid?
- **Strengths:** What did you contribute to a recent result? What can you deliver now
  or obtain within your limits? Separate reported ability from demonstrated outcomes.
- **Incidents:** What happened last time, what was tried, and what consequence followed?
- **Preferences:** Which tradeoff or mechanism fits how you want to work? Reuse the
  stated income goal; ask about geography or solo delivery only if it affects viability.

Follower counts do not prove reach, trust, buyer composition, or purchasing intent.
Access and expertise can develop; do not infer them from a market's attractiveness.
Teaching or speaking can support many models and does not automatically select education.
A hobby can stay a hobby; lifelong passion is unnecessary. A founder's successful
workflow improvement demonstrates capability, not recurring customer pain or a market.

## Pace and closure

Aim for about 6-8 user replies from the opening, closing sooner when ready. Table selection
and edits replace intermediate confirmation loops. Present the full gate and refined
sentence once for final confirmation; follow `rpt-interview.md` for closure and edits.
Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just ask in the conversation language.
If the user signals time pressure, asks to shorten, or asks for the result, deliver
the provisional close in that same reply: literal-template sentence, one honest verdict
with brief six-point findings/unknowns, and one concrete outside-chat action. This
overrides intake, table selection, and confirmation; no further questions or side-artifact-only
reply. Preserve the no-credible-idea exception rather than inventing a sentence.
At roughly eight replies, unresolved work becomes a provisional result and one concrete
outside-chat action, not another intake cycle. No credible idea is an honest outcome.
Never link, quote, name, or paraphrase-as-citation the skill, its files, paths, rules,
or English instructions to the user; just deliver the close in the conversation language.

## Gender-neutral language

In Polish and other gendered languages, use gender-neutral forms for the user until
their gender is known. Prefer present tense, infinitives, or impersonal questions;
do not infer gender from a name, job, or writing style. Keep assistant self-reference
neutral and consistent, for example "Sprawdzono" or "Sprawdźmy", rather than gendered
past tense. Preserve the literal mission templates unchanged.
