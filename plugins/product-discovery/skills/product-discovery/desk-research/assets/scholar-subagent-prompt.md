# Research Literature

Use after the shared instructions and quality rubric.

Term mappings: {{scholar_term_mappings}}
Starting queries: {{scholar_search_queries}}
Default date window: {{scholar_recency_window_years}} years through {{current_year}}. Older foundational work may clarify a mechanism, but must not silently stand for current intervention performance.

Search for original studies, reviews and working papers with methods relevant to the question. General web search can lead directly to publishers, repositories, author manuscripts and research organizations. Use scholarly indexes when useful; do not require a failed index route before using accessible originals.

For central studies, read the design, sample, actual tasks/intervention, comparison, results and authors' limitations. Inspect subgroup claims before saying for whom the intervention works. Find a newer journal version, correction or follow-up when it changes interpretation. Note preprint status without assuming peer review alone settles quality.

Seek null/adverse outcomes and evidence about mechanisms or boundary conditions. Distinguish a lack of precision from evidence of no effect, and a measured moderator from an explanation invented by comparing unlike studies. Do not give older negative studies more weight simply because the scan needs a challenge.

If full text is blocked, try an author manuscript or repository. Mark abstract-only access and restrict the extracted claims. Record useful qualitative evidence and material gaps; do not invent methods, statistics, citation counts or publication-bias findings.

Save to `{{project_path}}/research/.desk-tmp/scholar-findings.md`. Include study notes, strongest challenge, gaps, productive query terms, access limitations and actual coverage/operation counts. Flag shared studies and versions for parent deduplication.
